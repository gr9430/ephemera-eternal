Say ‘uncle’ — surrender to good times at Uncle Lou’s
Just when you thought tall boys of Colt 45 and bars with character were long lost to this town, along comes a place like Uncle Lou’s Entertainment Hall on Mills Avenue.
With strings of lights hanging from the ceiling, posters of Bob Marley, Janet Jackson and Madonna adorning the walls and Uncle Lou behind the bar, asking “What you would like?” in his flowing Jamaican accent, you know Orlando hasn’t lost all its local charm to the mighty martini bar.
Uncle Lou moved the bar, formerly located on Michigan Avenue, to Mills because the lease was up, and he thought this would be a better spot. Turns out he might be right — for all of us.
The night I stopped by, R&B; was streaming out of the sound system while I played a game of electric darts. (Note: This isn’t the sturdiest of stands so pull the darts out of the board with caution, kids). Another couple played a game of pool while breaking between shots to slow dance to the music. But it’s not all strictly R&B; — hip-hop, reggae, punk and rock acts make an eclectic mix of music, and we like that a lot.
It’s not only the mix of music that helps make this place a nice change from the cookie-cutter bars being churned out here. Every Thursday, the bar hosts a spoken-word night beginning at 7 p.m. that’s free to the public; Saturday night, there will be a pool and spade tournament in which the winner gets $150; finish second and your bar tab is paid.
Now I’m sure I don’t have to tell you, if you’ve read this far, that this is a very casual joint — you get that everywhere you turn in the small space, right down to the barstool I was sitting on, which left some kind of residue on the back of my jeans. The full bar has liquor, but the bottom-shelf location says a lot for the hangover you’ll have the next morning if you drink it. But there is a big beer selection — I stuck with a $3 Yueng-ling, although I was tempted to grab that $2 big boy I mentioned.
The bar also offers $5 martinis, and I doubt they’re served in a glass with a cookie-crust rim.
And that’s a good thing, because Uncle Lou’s is the nicotine-filled cloud of cigar smoke this town desperately needs.
Uncle Lou’s is at 1016 N. Mills Ave. in Orlando. Call 407-898-0009.

tips & trips
Like an aging Hollywood starlet, it seems the Club at Firestone can’t keep itself from going under the knife. On Thursday, the club will have a ribbon-cutting ceremony for yet another redesign, this time for a new upstairs “loft, den and patio.” The event starts at 9 p.m.; admission is $10, and it’s two-for-one drinks until midnight. Call 407-872-0066.
Forgot to include this in my Parliament House update last week, kids, but it sounds too good to miss. On Wednesday, Parliament will start a new weekly event called Drag Races. Five audience members do shots, get dressed up in women’s clothes and then have to perform a song for the audience. According to the press release “it’s either their star quality or their ability to keep their breasts up that helps determine the winner (the crowd is the judge).” The reference to “drag” makes me think men are the target here, but Parliament welcomes all so I say the ladies should take a turn as well and let the best man/woman take the prize. Call 407-425-7571.
